01 · About

Looking at cosmic growth through energetic light.

I am a doctoral student in astronomy at Peking University, advised by Prof. Xuebing Wu. I received my B.S. from Nanjing University in 2025 and was a visiting research student at Penn State in 2024.

My work uses X-ray and multi-wavelength observations to understand active galactic nuclei and black-hole accretion. I also contribute to the Einstein Probe FXT all-sky X-ray source catalog.

02 · Research

Three scales, one question: how do black holes grow?

From the inner accretion flow to the large-scale environments of the earliest quasars.

01

X-ray astronomy

Spectral and variability studies of AGN using sensitive observations from Chandra, XMM-Newton, NuSTAR, Swift, and Einstein Probe.

High-energy universe
02

Quasars & accretion

Uncovering the physical nature of weak-line quasars and the unusual accretion flows that power their extreme luminosities.

Inner accretion flow
03

Obscured & distant AGN

Finding heavily obscured low-mass AGN and testing how galaxy mergers and dense high-redshift environments shape black-hole growth.

Cosmic evolution
